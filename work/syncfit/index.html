SyncFit at a glance

SyncFit is an app with three main parts.

  1. Find your workout partner

    Suggests people nearby who train at similar times and have similar goals.

  2. Workout tracking

    Tracks your workouts and shows your progress.

  3. Local events

    Shows fitness events near you on a map.

The problem

A lot of people, like me, enjoy working out with someone. It keeps us motivated. But finding the right person isn’t easy. When I moved to a new city, I couldn’t find anyone who matched my schedule. I wanted to train in the morning, but my friends’ schedules didn’t match mine. I didn’t want to train alone. That’s where the idea for SyncFit came from.

Key problems

  1. 🤝

    No one to train with

    People's schedules and workouts rarely match, so it's hard to find the right person.

  2. 🔋

    Motivation and company

    A workout feels easier when someone is there to keep you company.

  3. 🏋️

    Different fitness levels

    Some people feel intimidated by someone much fitter, while others want a more experienced partner to learn from.

  4. 🛡️

    Safety when meeting strangers

    Meeting someone new to work out with can feel unsafe, especially for women.

Studying similar apps

I studied apps people already use to meet people, track workouts and find what's nearby.

🤝 Finding people

  • Strava

🗺️ Maps

  • Google Maps
  • Snapchat

There's no well-known app just for finding a workout partner. Small apps like GymBuddy, Gymly and Spot Gym Buddy try, but have very few ratings.

Dating apps like Bumble, Tinder and Hinge solve a similar problem, meeting someone new, so I studied how they do it.

What I took from them

  1. 🤝From dating apps

    Bumble used to let only women send the first message, and Tinder checks that a selfie matches your photos. These shaped the app's one-message rule and face verification.

  2. 📈From tracking apps

    Apple Watch shows a summary when you end a workout, and WHOOP shows each workout's effort and heart rate zones, so the app ends every workout with a clear summary.

  3. 📍From map apps

    The Community tab uses a map like Snapchat's, but it shows fitness events, not where people are.

  4. 🔗Across all

    No app I studied was made for finding a workout partner, tracking workouts and finding fitness events together. The app brings all three together, with safety built in.

Key findings

From the survey and the apps I looked at, these were the main things I learned.

  1. 🤝

    Wanting a workout partner was common, and most common among younger people.

  2. 🗓️

    Different schedules were the main reason people couldn't find a partner. People also wanted a partner who does the same workouts.

  3. 🛡️

    People said a verified profile would make them feel safer. They also wanted to know what workouts someone does before reaching out.

  4. 📍

    Many people find it hard to find local fitness events, and they want to know the type, place, time and cost before joining.

Events near you

The Community tab shows fitness events near you on a map, from morning group runs to big races like the Bengaluru Marathon. Tap an event to see a quick preview, then open it for the full details: where to meet, the date and time, and what to expect. If you want to join, you can register right in the app, review your details, pay, and get your race pass.

What changed along the way

These are some changes I made after family and friends tried the prototype, along with a few iterations of my own. Drag the slider to compare before and after.

3 changes
  1. BeforeAfter

    Making onboarding skippable

    People who tested said sign-up took too long. I kept only the must-have steps and moved the fitness questions to later, so you can start using the app sooner.

  2. BeforeAfter

    Simplifying the activity screen

    In the earlier version, each workout's numbers were listed in rows. Now they sit in small cards.

  3. BeforeAfter

    Picking a workout: from icons to cards

    Another version showed the workouts as round icons. The final cards add each workout's type and calorie range.

What I chose not to build

  • 📍Live details of who is at each sessionShowing who is at which session, and when, shares too much information about people.

    Instead Events only show how many people have registered.

  • 🔔Accountability remindersThe idea was that the app reminds you and your partner to show up. It would only work if people told the app who they trained with, and most wouldn't.

    Instead The app keeps you going with your own progress.

  • 📱A social feedA feed could motivate some people, but it would turn the app into another place to scroll.

    Instead You stay motivated by your own badges and progress, not other people's posts.

What I learned

Looking back, here’s what this project taught me.

  1. ⚡

    Shorter onboarding works better

    The first version asked everything at the start, and testers found it too long. Asking only for the essentials made it easier to get started.

  2. 🛡️

    Safety comes first

    Meeting strangers is the riskiest part of the idea, so I added face verification and the one-message rule from the start.

  3. ✂️

    Leaving features out

    Reminders and live session details both sounded useful, but they took the app away from its main purpose: finding a workout partner.

  4. 🔍

    Research shaped the core features

    The survey showed that different schedules and feeling safe mattered most, so matching by workout time and verified profiles became the core of the app.

  5. 🚀

    What's next

    Testing the "set up later" path properly, and adding a way to share your live location with a trusted contact during a meet-up.
