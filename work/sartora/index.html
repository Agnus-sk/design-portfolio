Sartora at a glance

Sartora is an app with three main parts, A digital wardrobe, An AI stylist, & A laundry service.

  1. A digital wardrobe

    Add clothes by taking photos, and Sartora organizes them into categories in a digital wardrobe, making the entire collection easy to browse.

  2. An AI stylist

    Luna, the AI stylist, suggests outfits based on the clothes you already own. You can also ask Luna for outfit ideas for a specific occasion, whether it’s work, dinner, or a trip.

  3. A laundry service

    Book laundry pickup, check the estimated price beforehand, and track your order status until delivery.

The problem

Getting dressed every day takes more time and effort than it should. Most mornings, I stood in front of my wardrobe, not sure what to wear, and wished something could help me decide. That’s where the idea for Sartora came from.

Key problems

  1. ⏳

    Choosing an outfit takes time

    Picking an outfit and matching it takes time, especially on busy mornings.

  2. 🧠

    People forget what they own

    Clothes at the back of the wardrobe get forgotten, so people buy similar ones again.

  3. 🔁

    The same outfits get repeated

    People wear what they can easily see, and style it the same way every time.

  4. 🧺

    Laundry takes time too

    Laundry takes up a lot of time, especially on busy days.

Studying similar apps

🧺 Laundry

  • Laundryheap
  • Rinse

What I took from them

  1. 👗From styling apps

    Styling apps already suggest outfits, so the app focuses on the clothes you own and keeps setup light.

  2. 🧺From laundry apps

    Most laundry apps charge by the weight of your clothes, not per piece. The app works the same way, and it shows the weighed price before you pay.

  3. 🔗Across both

    None of them put styling and laundry in one place.

Key findings

From the survey and the apps I tried, these were the main things I learned.

  1. 👗

    Most people have enough clothes but run out of outfit ideas.

  2. 📱

    People find a digital wardrobe useful, but they don't want to set it up before using the app.

  3. 📌

    Most people look for outfit ideas on Pinterest. But it's hard to recreate those looks with the clothes they already have.

  4. 🧺

    Most people don't use a laundry service. The ones who do are worried about the price.

What changed along the way

These are some changes I made after family and friends tried the prototype, along with a few iterations of my own. Drag the slider to compare before and after.

3 changes
  1. BeforeAfter

    Choosing a bag size instead of counting clothes

    Counting every piece of clothing was too slow. Now you pick a bag size, and the price is checked by weight.

  2. BeforeAfter

    Explaining the "Wear this" button

    Testers didn't know why they should tap "Wear this". A short line now explains that it stops Luna from suggesting the same outfit again.

  3. BeforeAfter

    Showing today's outfit straight away

    The "Get outfit plan" button was replaced with today's outfit, shown as soon as the app opens.

What I chose not to build

  • 🏷️Tracking each garment in the laundryLaundry partners weigh clothes together, and families often send theirs in one bag, so single pieces can't be tracked.

    Instead You choose a bag size, and the app tracks the whole order until delivery.

  • 👥A community feedIt would bring the focus back to other people's clothes.

    Instead Luna suggests outfits from the clothes you already own.

  • 📅Weekly pickup plansOnly a few people in the survey wanted regular pickups.

    Instead You book a single pickup and choose a time slot that suits you.

What I learned

Looking back, here’s what this project taught me.

  1. 🔄

    Research changed my plan

    I first planned the digital wardrobe as the main feature. The survey showed people cared more about outfit ideas, so the wardrobe became a supporting feature.

  2. 🎯

    Keeping the scope in check

    The laundry flow grew into the biggest part of the app. I had to keep it useful without letting it take over the styling features.

  3. ✏️

    Next time

    I would test with people outside my friends and family, and give them specific tasks to complete.

  4. 🚀

    What's next

    Next, I'd let people check how the AI has labelled their clothes, so a wrong label doesn't lead to a wrong outfit suggestion.
